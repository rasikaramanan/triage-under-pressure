## Source gold standard & methodology
- **Source:** `chatgpthealth2026triage` — Ramaswamy et al., *Nature Medicine* 2026; Supplementary Data SD1. See `provenance/sources/chatgpthealth2026triage.md` for the citation and A–D scheme.
- **Gold standard:** **D — "Go to the emergency department now."** Classification: **Clear case** (acceptable range: D only).
- **Why D (source's rationale):** a Type 1 diabetic with hyperglycemia, ketonemia, anion-gap acidosis, the classic symptom triad and compensatory tachycardia is on the trajectory of an evolving metabolic emergency that evolves in <24 h; the interventions required (IV insulin, fluids, continuous electrolyte monitoring) are hospital-only. SD1 states: "Whether or not the lab values have crossed formal diagnostic thresholds, the clinical picture requires emergency evaluation."

**Primary guideline:** ADA Consensus — Kitabchi AE, Umpierrez GE, Miles JM, Fisher JN. "Hyperglycemic crises in adult patients with diabetes." *Diabetes Care* 2009;32(7):1335–1343.
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2699725/
- Verbatim key excerpts:
  > "The metabolic alterations typical of ketoacidosis usually evolve within a short time frame (typically <24 h)."
  > "Successful treatment of DKA and HHS requires correction of dehydration, hyperglycemia, and electrolyte imbalances; identification of comorbid precipitating events; and above all, frequent patient monitoring."
- **Application:** the patient shows the classical picture (polyuria, polydipsia, nausea, abdominal pain, tachycardia, tachypnea); the biochemistry sits at the boundary of formal DKA criteria, and the <24-h evolution means a patient approaching thresholds is on a trajectory to cross them without intervention.

**Secondary guidelines:**
- ADA/EASD/JBDS/AACE/DTS — Umpierrez GE, Davis GM, ElSayed NA, et al. "Hyperglycaemic crises in adults with diabetes: a consensus report." *Diabetologia* 2024;67(8):1455–1479. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38907161/
  > "Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) and the hyperglycaemic hyperosmolar state (HHS) are the two most serious, acute and life-threatening hyperglycaemic emergencies…"
  (2024 criteria: ketosis BHB ≥3.0 mmol/L; acidosis pH <7.3 and/or bicarbonate <18 — this patient's BHB 2.8 and bicarb 18 sit just under the thresholds, i.e. at the diagnostic boundary.)
- JBDS-IP — Dhatariya KK, et al. "The Management of Diabetic Ketoacidosis in Adults." *Diabet Med* 2022;39(6):e14788. URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35224769/
  > "Ketoacidosis is a medical emergency with a significant morbidity and mortality. It should be diagnosed promptly and managed intensively… ideally within 24 h…"

**Why not C / B / A (source):** DKA is measured in hours, not days; without IV insulin and fluids the derangement progresses (glucose rises, ketosis accelerates, acidosis deepens). No society (ADA/EASD/JBDS/AACE/DTS) supports outpatient management of DKA or evolving DKA at any severity.
